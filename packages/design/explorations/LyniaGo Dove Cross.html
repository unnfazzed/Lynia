The dove · hidden cross studies

Noah’s dove, no leaf. In each study the cross hides where paper naturally creases — visible only when you know. All use the same tilted-cross construction: the long arm follows the flight axis, the short arm crosses at the upper third (latin proportions).

3a · the crease cross

Cross in the fold

The body’s main crease is the long arm; a second crease crosses it at the upper third. To anyone else: origami folds. To you: the cross carries the bird.

3b · the tail cross

Cross in the keel

The whole cross lives in the darker tail fold — the weight the bird flies with. Quietest of the four; disappears entirely at small sizes.

3c · the shoulder cross

Cross at the wing

The wing-gap is one arm; a short crease cuts through the shoulder — the cross sits exactly where the wing takes the load. “It flies because of what it carries.”

3d · the morning star

Star before the beak (replaces the leaf)

A four-point star — a cross of light — exactly where the olive leaf was. Reads as a sparkle guiding the bird; it is a cross to those who look.
