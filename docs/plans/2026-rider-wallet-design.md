## Problem Statement

Lynia's revenue model is decided (CONCEPT §6): a rider-side commission on each completed
parcel delivery, collected **prepaid** from a rider-funded balance — the only collection
mechanism with zero credit risk in a cash market where the app moves no customer money.
Phase 1 (policy layer, `COMMISSION` in `packages/shared/src/policy.ts`, admin overview,
honest UI copy) is landed. The wallet itself does not exist: no balance, no ledger, no
top-ups, no low-balance gate. This design covers that build: riders deposit via Zimbabwe
mobile money (EcoCash / InnBucks / O'mari), each completed ride debits
`perRideCommission(agreedFare)` (eventually 5%), and a balance floor gates riders from
going online until they top up.

## Demand Evidence

- The commission model itself was validated in a prior office-hours pass and recorded as
  DECIDED in CONCEPT §6 and the risk register (item 2) — this session did not re-litigate it.
- Demand for the *wallet* is derived, not direct: it is the infrastructure the decided
  model requires. It launches at 0% commission, so no rider demand for top-ups exists
  until the rate flips — which is precisely why it ships inert first (see Premise 5).
- Open demand check (assignment): whether pilot-corridor riders hold **USD** mobile-money
  balances. If their EcoCash liquidity is ZiG, top-ups fail at the rail regardless of
  integration quality.

## Status Quo

Without this build, commission collection has no mechanism at all: the earlier post-paid
weekly cash-settlement engine was deliberately removed (superseded by the prepaid model —
see `docs/plans/2026-biker-prepaid-commission.md`), and the admin console shows a
read-only commission overview that bills nothing. If the rate flipped today, Lynia would
have to chase riders for cash weekly — per-rider credit risk, collection ops, and exactly
the model the team already rejected as wrong for a cash, low-trust market.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**User:** the Harare pilot rider — motorbike courier, cash economy, EcoCash as the
dominant (near-universal) mobile-money rail, price-sensitive, hard to recruit (cold-start
rider supply is risk-register #1). They keep 100% of the agreed fare today and were
promised 0% commission for the launch period.

**Wedge (as decided in this session):** the full wallet ships in one build — ledger,
per-ride debit, low-balance gate, admin manual credits, **and direct EcoCash top-up
integration** — but launches with `ratePct: 0`, so no rider feels anything until the flip
is deliberately made. The founder explicitly chose to include the EcoCash integration in
this build (D5) rather than defer it, accepting the Econet-onboarding coupling in
exchange for having the complete end-state with no follow-on phase to schedule.

## Constraints

- **Cold-start:** nothing in this build may add friction to rider recruitment while
  commission is 0% — no forced top-up, no wallet step in onboarding.
- **Cash market:** the app moves no customer money; the wallet touches rider money only.
- **Regulatory:** avoid RBZ stored-value/money-transmission exposure — hence the
  non-withdrawable float (Premise 1).
- **External dependency:** direct EcoCash API access requires an Econet merchant
  agreement; its onboarding timeline is the build's critical path (founder-accepted).
- **Existing schema:** money is `Decimal(10,2)` USD matching `Order.agreedFare`; the
  dormant `Settlement` table awaits one destructive migration.
- **Ops capacity:** manual credit path assumes ops can turn around a credit in minutes
  during pilot hours — fine at 5–15 riders, a real SLA question at hundreds.

## Premises (all agreed 2026-07-13)

1. **Commission float, not a general wallet.** Deposit and spend on commission only; no
   in-app withdrawals (refunds are an admin action). Keeps clear of RBZ stored-value
   territory and removes float-fraud surface.
2. **USD only.** Ledger, top-ups, and commission are USD, matching `Order.currency`.
   ZiG is out of scope until product decides otherwise.
3. **Debit at completion, never blocking.** `perRideCommission(agreedFare)` is written in
   the same DB transaction that marks the ride completed. A debit may take the balance
   below the floor — and below zero (5% of a $50 fare is $2.50, more than the $2 floor's
   headroom). **Negative balances are permitted**, displayed to the rider as owed, and
   netted by the next top-up; this deliberately supersedes the plan doc's "can never go
   negative" claim, because the alternative is blocking a delivered parcel. The gate
   stops the *next* ride, never a parcel already delivered.
4. **Direct EcoCash integration in this build** (founder call, D5 — upheld against the
   cross-model challenge). No Paynow / aggregator (founder call, D2). InnBucks and
   O'mari are served by the manual admin-credit path at launch. Econet merchant
   onboarding starts immediately as the critical path.
5. **Ships at 0%; 5% is a later, deliberate flip.** The **flip itself** (changing
   `ratePct` in production) is explicitly *not* part of this build's definition of done.
   The transition **playbook and tooling** (grace credits, bulk seed-credit action,
   rehearsal test — see Flow 5) *are* in this build, so the flip is executable when
   product decides.
6. **The dormant `Settlement` table is dropped** in this build's migration. **AMENDED
   2026-07-16 (DOC-16-06, wallet & data-lifecycle audit):** not executed in PR1 —
   migration `0027_commission_wallet/migration.sql` explicitly deferred it ("left in
   place... its drop is a separate destructive migration once the wallet has soaked").
   The wallet has since soaked through PR1 + WD-001..011 + BH-07..12 + WD-012..015 with
   zero reads/writes against `Settlement`/`Refund.settlementId` the whole time, but a
   drop is a destructive migration with no functional upside (the table is dead weight,
   not a live risk) — deferred again, deliberately, rather than bundled into an
   unrelated bug-fix PR. Tracked as `KB-SETTLEMENT-DROP` in `docs/KNOWN_BUGS.md`; the
   next refactor-routine run (dead-code removal is explicitly in its remit) is the
   right owner for the actual `DROP TABLE` migration.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent (fresh context, structured summary only) reviewed the
concept:

- **Steelman:** in a cash market with no in-app payment leg, a prepaid float is the only
  commission mechanism with zero credit risk and zero collection ops; building it now at
  0% gives the ledger, idempotent debit, and gate months of production soak while stakes
  are zero; the non-withdrawable float stays under the RBZ payment-services line.
- **Key insight (adopted):** "flip to 5% later via a one-line policy change" hides the
  real work. On flip day every rider's balance is $0 — below the $2
  `lowBalanceBlockBelow` floor — so the one-line change **would instantly gate the entire
  fleet offline**, against a risk register whose #1 item is rider supply. The transition
  (seeded grace credits, pre-flip top-up campaign, comms, possibly cohort staging) is
  part of the system and is now in scope as flip-day tooling.
- **Challenged premise:** #4 — it argued the EcoCash integration code should be a
  pre-flip follow-on phase (manual credits cover pilot scale; code written now rots
  unexercised at 0% while Econet onboarding blocks the ship date). **The founder heard
  the challenge and kept the integration in this build** — a deliberate trade of ship-date
  coupling for a complete end-state with no follow-on phase to schedule. Mitigation
  retained from the challenge: the wallet core does not wait for Econet — see the
  sequencing note in Recommended Approach.
- **48-hour prototype suggestion:** ledger + manual rail + flip-day rehearsal
  (bulk seed-credit, end-to-end test at `ratePct: 5` in a test env), zero payment APIs.
  This survives as the *internal sequencing* of the chosen approach, not its scope.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Straight-line build (CHOSEN)
Everything wired directly: wallet tables per the existing plan-doc sketch, debit hook in
both completion paths, gate reason, admin credit + bulk seed-credit, rider UI, and an
EcoCash C2B client hardwired into a single wallet module with a `TopUp` table.
Effort M (human ~1.5–2 wks / CC ~1–2 days) + Econet lead time. Risk Low-Med.
Pros: fastest complete build, smallest diff, one traceable module.
Cons (accepted): EcoCash client hardwired — automating InnBucks/O'mari later means
reworking the top-up flow; provider-statement reconciliation deferred; stuck-pending
handling is simple polling + expiry rather than a full state machine.

### Approach B: Rail-adapter wallet platform
Same scope behind a `PaymentRailAdapter` interface (matching the repo's D7 adapter
convention), `TopUp` as a full state machine with BullMQ expiry, daily reconciliation
job. Effort L. Pros: InnBucks/O'mari become drop-ins; stuck payments and reconciliation
designed-in. Cons: ~a week more effort for near-zero top-up volume until the flip;
speculative abstraction until a second automated rail exists. **Not chosen** — the
founder prefers the leaner build now and accepts the later refactor cost knowingly.

### Approach C: Double-entry mini-ledger
Balanced journal entries across rider liability accounts and a Lynia revenue account.
Audit-grade by construction; heaviest to build and contribute to; rigor a one-float
pilot cannot exercise. **Not chosen.**

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A — straight-line build**, with the following shape (founder-selected over the
reviewer-recommended B; the divergence and its accepted costs are recorded above).

**Data model** (new Prisma models; money `Decimal(10,2)` USD; drop `Settlement` in the
same migration — which also drops `Refund.settlementId` and its index, the one remaining
reference to the settlement era):

- `CommissionAccount` — 1:1 with `Rider`: `riderId` PK/FK, `balance` (default 0),
  `currency` ("USD"), `updatedAt`. Cached running total. **No status column** (eng review
  OV-4A): a wallet freeze is a `Rider.heldReason: "commission_freeze"` hold —
  admin-clear-only exactly like the RH-01 `velocity` pattern, never self-cleared by score
  recovery — and top-up endpoints check that same single authority. Rows are **lazily
  upserted** on first touch (eng review 1A) so "account missing" is not a reachable
  failure inside the completion transaction. Balance writes take a row lock (`SELECT …
  FOR UPDATE` on the account row) inside every ledger-writing transaction so concurrent
  debit/credit keeps `balance` and `balanceAfter` consistent; when the rider row is also
  locked, lock order is always rider → account.
- `CommissionLedger` — append-only: `id`, `riderId`, `orderId?`, `topUpId?`, `type`
  (`topup` | `ride_commission` | `adjustment` | `reversal`), signed `amount`,
  `balanceAfter`, `ratePct?` (the rate a `ride_commission` was charged at — eng review
  OV-2A), `idemKey?` (**unique** when present — eng review OV-3A: manual credits carry a
  key minted when the admin form opens; bulk seed credits carry `(campaignId, riderId)`
  so a re-run no-ops per rider), `note`, `actor` (system | admin profileId), `createdAt`;
  index `(riderId, createdAt)` for history + the integrity job.
  **Unique `(riderId, orderId, type)`** makes the per-ride debit idempotent under retries
  — and stays absolute: exactly ONE `ride_commission` row per order, ever (see fare-adjust
  below). The debit itself is **check-then-insert under the account row lock** (eng
  review 1A), so the constraint is a backstop, not a control-flow mechanism — no
  unique-violation path can abort the completion transaction.
- `TopUp` — `id`, `riderId`, `rail` (`ecocash` | `innbucks` | `omari` | `manual`),
  `amount`, `providerRef?` (unique — EcoCash transaction id or admin idempotency key),
  `status` (`pending` | `confirmed` | `failed` | `expired`), `initiatedAt`, `resolvedAt?`.
  Confirmation is exactly-once by construction: the credit transaction does a CAS on
  `TopUp.status` (`pending | expired → confirmed` — `expired` is re-openable so a late
  provider confirmation still credits; `failed` and `confirmed` are terminal; zero rows
  updated = already processed, abort) and `CommissionLedger.topUpId` is **unique**, so a
  replayed confirmation can neither re-transition the top-up nor insert a second ledger
  row.

**Flows:**

1. **Debit on completion** — in both completion paths in
   `apps/api/src/orders/order-lifecycle.service.ts` (customer-rates path and auto-close
   `completeOrder`), inside the existing completion transaction: compute
   `perRideCommission(agreedFare)`; at 0 skip; else write the `ride_commission` ledger
   row and decrement the balance. `agreedFare` is nullable in the schema — a completed
   order should always have one, so a null here **skips the debit and raises an alert**
   (observability event), never throws. Never blocks completion; balance may cross the
   floor and go negative (Premise 3).
2. **Low-balance gate** — new `commission_low_balance` reason in the rider online-gate
   system (mobile `gates.ts` + API online-gate check): balance `<
   COMMISSION.lowBalanceBlockBelow` blocks going online / accepting, with a "Top up to
   keep riding" state; clears when balance returns to the floor or above. At 0% with a
   $0 starting balance the gate MUST NOT fire — the guard is literal:
   `COMMISSION.ratePct > 0 && balance < COMMISSION.lowBalanceBlockBelow`, so the pilot
   is untouched.
3. **EcoCash top-up (automated)** — rider enters amount (≥ `minTopUp`) → API creates
   `TopUp(pending)` → EcoCash C2B charge request pushes a USSD approval prompt to the
   rider's handset → confirmation via Econet callback and/or status polling. **A callback
   never credits directly**: it is treated as a hint that triggers a server-side status
   query against the EcoCash API (or must carry a verified signature, per Econet's spec),
   and the credit is written only from the verified result — an unauthenticated credit
   webhook is a free-money hole. Pending top-ups are polled by a BullMQ repeatable job
   (same job infra as offer expiry) and marked `expired` on timeout; the rider can retry,
   and a late confirmation on an expired top-up still credits exactly once via the CAS +
   unique `topUpId` mechanism above.
4. **Manual credit (launch rail for InnBucks/O'mari, fallback for EcoCash)** — rider
   pays Lynia's merchant account off-app; admin records a credit in the console
   (creates `TopUp(rail, providerRef=idempotency key, confirmed)` + ledger row, full
   audit trail with `actor`). **Abuse controls (eng review OV-3A):** the idempotency key
   is minted when the admin form OPENS (stable across submit retries, so a double-click
   is structurally harmless), credits are capped per entry (env-tunable, default $50),
   and the endpoint is restricted to an allowed admin role.
5. **Flip-day tooling (in scope now, used later)** — admin **bulk seed-credit** action
   (grace credit as `adjustment` rows to all active riders, **campaign-keyed**: each row's
   `idemKey = (campaignId, riderId)`, so a timeout-and-retry re-run no-ops per rider —
   eng review OV-3A), and a documented flip playbook: seed credits → in-app + WhatsApp
   comms → flip the rate → monitor gate counts. **The flip itself is an env change, not
   a deploy** (eng review OV-2A): `COMMISSION_RATE_PCT` env overrides the `policy.ts`
   constant (same pattern as `SOS_POLICY.safetyLineEnv` / `BROADCAST.baseRadiusEnv`),
   validated at startup (clamped, asserted). The mobile release carrying the
   `commission_low_balance` gate-reason mapping ships **before** any flip so old
   binaries never show a bare generic error to gated riders. The rehearsal in a test
   environment exercises the **actual flip operation** — seed-credit, then env flip,
   then completion → debit → gate → credit → clear — and is part of this build's
   definition of done.
   **Flip firing conditions (CEO review 2026-07-13, D3/1A + OV#3 — defaults to
   recalibrate against shadow-accrual data, not vows):** (1) sustained ≥50 completed
   rides/week for 4 consecutive weeks AND (2) ≥20 weekly-active riders AND (3)
   **projected monthly commission (measured by shadow accrual) exceeds estimated
   monthly rail + ops cost by a clear margin** — fee inputs pinned from the Econet
   agreement when it lands; never flip into negative-margin collection. Decision
   owner = founder; rider notice = **14 days** of in-app + WhatsApp comms before the
   first deduction, and the comms state the **all-in cost honestly** (nominal 5% plus
   top-up/cash-in fees and IMTT — riders will compute the effective rate themselves;
   disclosure beats discovery); grace credit = **$5 per active rider** (= `minTopUp`,
   ≈ the first ~33 rides' commission at the ~$3 median fare). The flip is a planned
   business event, not a mood.
   **Wallet reveal (CEO review OV#5):** rider-facing wallet surfaces (balance card,
   ledger, top-up entry) ship **hidden behind a server-driven visibility flag** in the
   same config payload as the rate (2A) — they appear when the 14-day flip comms
   begin, together with the grace credit and the announcement: one coherent reveal,
   not a silent ominous tab during recruitment. **Superseded (dated product decision
   2026-07-15):** `WALLET_REVEAL` now defaults `"true"` — the wallet is shown from
   launch even at 0% commission (`env.ts:183-189`); the flag remains as a kill-switch,
   not the default-hidden gate this paragraph originally specified. Admin surfaces, the
   ledger, shadow
   metrics and every backend path are live from day one regardless; internal/test
   riders can be flagged visible earlier to exercise the UI.
   **Collection-integrity metrics (CEO review OV#2):** the metrics layer also tracks
   declared-fare distribution over time, repeat-pair average-fare trend (does a
   matched customer–rider pair's declared fare fall after the flip?), and matched-pair
   ride-frequency post-flip — disintermediation and fare under-declaration are the
   known failure mode of rider-side commission in cash markets; these dashboards are
   how leakage shows up in data instead of in a revenue shortfall. Measurement only —
   enforcement responses are future work, decided when there's something to respond to.
6. **Integrity check** — a scheduled job asserting `sum(ledger) == balance` per account,
   alerting on drift. (Provider-statement reconciliation deferred, per Approach A.)

**Surfaces:** rider earnings screen gains balance + ledger history + top-up entry point
(EcoCash form; instruction card for manual rails) + blocked-gate state; admin rider
detail gains balance/ledger view, manual credit form, and the bulk seed-credit action.
**Show-the-math ledger rows (CEO review 2026-07-13, D4/2A):** every rider-visible
debit row renders a checkable receipt — amount, the rate and fare it derives from, and
the ride it belongs to (e.g. "−$0.15 · 5% of $3.00 · delivery to Avondale · Tue 14:02")
— all from fields the ledger already stores (`amount`, `ratePct`, `orderId`,
`createdAt`). Credits show rail + reference. A rider must be able to reconcile any
deduction against a ride they remember without contacting support; disputes become
lookups, not arguments.

**Journey storyboard (emotional arc):** (1) *Reveal* — wallet appears WITH the flip
announcement; first open shows the $5 grace credit already there: "We've added $5 to
get you started — commission starts [date]." First feeling must be *given*, not
*charged*. (2) *First debit* — receipt row shows the math; feeling: *checkable*. (3)
*Low balance* — warning threshold at floor+$1 (soft chip on Wallet, no interruption);
feeling: *warned, not ambushed*. (4) *Gated* — calm, specific, actionable (exact
amount needed, one tap to fix); feeling: *blocked by a rule, not punished*. (5)
*Cleared* — immediate, celebratory-but-quiet toast; feeling: *the system is fair*.

## Eng Review Decisions (2026-07-13, /plan-eng-review)

Locked interactively; each traces to a numbered finding in the review session.

1. **(1A) Debit is same-transaction and unfailable by design.** One shared
   `chargeCommission(tx, {orderId, riderId, agreedFare})` helper called from BOTH
   completion paths (`rate()` and `completeOrder()` in
   `apps/api/src/orders/order-lifecycle.service.ts`); `rate()`'s order select adds
   `agreedFare`. Account lazily upserted; idempotency via check-then-insert under the
   account row lock — no exception-driven control flow inside the transaction.
2. **(2A) The rate the rider sees is server-authoritative.** Wallet/earnings/gate API
   payloads carry `ratePct`, `lowBalanceBlockBelow`, and the balance; mobile renders
   server values only (bundled policy is offline-fallback copy). Rationale:
   `apps/mobile/app/earnings/index.tsx:100` — the bundled constant only changes on app
   update, which propagates in days-to-weeks locally.
3. **(3A) The gate input is compile-time required.** `onlineRefusalReason`
   (`apps/api/src/riders/online-gate.ts`) gains required commission fields
   (`commissionBalance`, active-rate flag) plus a `commission_low_balance` refusal +
   `REFUSAL_MESSAGE` entry and the mobile `gates.ts` mapping — every call site
   (rider.service, matching/offers, tracking) fails to compile until its query supplies
   the balance. Call sites that evaluate many riders fetch the balance in the same batch
   query (no per-rider N+1).
4. **(4A + OV-1A) Fare-adjust auto-corrects commission via a single delta `adjustment`
   row** at the original ride's stored rate. See resolved Open Question 6.
5. **(OV-2A) The flip is an env operation.** `COMMISSION_RATE_PCT` overrides the
   constant; every `ride_commission` row stores its `ratePct`; pre-flip mobile release
   requirement; rehearsal exercises the real env flip. See Flow 5.
6. **(OV-3A) Credit-side idempotency + abuse controls.** Ledger `idemKey`, campaign-keyed
   bulk seed, form-open key provenance, per-credit cap, admin role restriction.
7. **(OV-4A) Single freeze authority.** No `CommissionAccount.status`; freeze =
   `heldReason: "commission_freeze"`, admin-clear-only (RH-01 velocity semantics); the
   `HeldReason` union grows to three values and every exhaustive switch over it is
   updated — the RH-01 self-clear logic explicitly treats the new value as
   non-score-clearable.
8. **(OV-5A) Shadow accrual during the 0% period.** `chargeCommission` computes the
   would-be commission at the calibration rate on every completion and emits it as a
   metric + structured log (never a ledger row) — real-traffic soak for the computation
   and measured data for calibrating the rate and the $2 floor.
9. **(D2) Delivery shape: two stacked PRs.** PR1 = wallet core (migration, debit, gate,
   manual credits, rider/admin UI, flip tooling, shadow accrual); PR2 = EcoCash rail
   (client, top-up flow, poller) rebased on PR1's interfaces.
10. **(Outside voice, rejected — recorded, not adopted, per explicit founder decision
    OV-6C):** DoD split per PR; refund-path corrections (issue-refund commission policy,
    documented manual float-refund procedure, `issues.service.ts` still writes `Refund`
    rows); payments-lawyer verification of the float premise; asking Econet in writing
    about USD (FCA) C2B support. The doc stands as approved on all four.

**Test requirements (from the coverage audit — written WITH the code, not after):**
the full matrix lives in the eng-review test plan artifact
(`~/.gstack/projects/unnfazzed-Lynia/root-claude-wallet-system-design-701zn9-eng-review-test-plan-20260713.md`),
consumed by `/qa`. Three **CRITICAL regression tests** are non-negotiable (iron rule —
the diff modifies existing behavior): (R1) both completion paths still complete orders
at 0% with wallet tables present, including the rider-less edge; (R2) every existing
online-gate refusal reason (kyc/banned/suspended/on_hold/cooldown) behaves identically
at every call site after the input-shape change; (R3) fare-adjust without commission
interaction (0-rate era) still works. All money paths target ★★★ (behavior + edge +
error); the E2E set is: top-up journey, gate journey, flip rehearsal, wallet-screen
offline.

**Sequencing inside the build (from the cross-model challenge, adopted):** land wallet
core (migration, debit, gate, manual credit, UI) first — it is fully shippable and
testable with admin-seeded balances while Econet onboarding pends; the EcoCash client
lands behind config (`ECOCASH_*` env; rail hidden in UI until configured) so the wallet
ship date never waits on the merchant agreement even though both are one build.

1. **USD vs ZiG rail liquidity** — do corridor riders hold USD EcoCash balances? (Part

6. **Refund interaction — RESOLVED at eng review (4A + OV-1A).** Commission follows
   fare-adjusts automatically: the fare-adjust transaction writes **one `adjustment`
   ledger row carrying the commission delta** (new commission minus old, computed at the
   **original ride's stored `ratePct`**, never the current rate). This keeps the
   one-`ride_commission`-row-per-order invariant absolute — no reversal-and-re-charge,
   so the idempotency constraint is never violated, and repeated adjusts just append
   deltas. `reversal` remains a **reserved** ledger type with no writer in this build.
