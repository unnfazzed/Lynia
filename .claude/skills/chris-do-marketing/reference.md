# Chris Do frameworks — reference

Summarised in our own words from Chris Do's public material (The Futur podcast, YouTube,
social posts, his *Unbland Yourself* personal-branding workbook). Several points come from
second-hand recaps of his talks; where the wording matters, check the original at
thefutur.com. Don't quote him verbatim and don't attribute words to him that aren't sourced.

## 1. Who: pick your client

- **Specific beats broad.** Serving a narrow, named audience earns more and is easier to
  market than trying to serve everyone. Underpricing and weak marketing are usually a
  positioning problem, not a pricing or a tactics problem.
- **Psychographics over demographics.** Describe the buyer by what they believe, value,
  fear and aspire to. That unlocks sharper offers, clearer messaging and higher prices.
- **Good clients have skin in the game.** Prefer decision-makers who are invested in the
  outcome; walk away from the overly risk-averse or the ones who only want the cheapest.

## 2. What: positioning and brand

- **Different, not better.** "Better" invites a comparison you'll lose on price.
  "Different" creates value only you can offer.
- **Own one word / one space.** A brand anchors on a single idea people associate with
  it. Owning a specific space matters more than being broadly good at many things.
- **Positioning line:** *We help [who] [achieve what] so that [why it matters].*
- **Unbland yourself.** A clear, consistent, fully aligned brand (what you say, how you
  look, what you do) is the defence against commoditisation — including by AI.
- **Story and emotion.** People remember stories and feelings, not feature lists. Lead
  with the customer's story; you are the guide, they are the hero.

## 3. Why: generosity as strategy

- **Give value away first.** The Futur grew by giving its knowledge away for free and
  selling depth, implementation and community later — trust compounds.
- Teach what you know. The fear that teaching "gives away the secret" is mostly
  unfounded; the people who can do it themselves were never your clients.

## 4. Content

- **Value first, pitch rarely.** Most posts should teach, reframe or inspire; a minority
  ask for anything.
- **Pillars.** Pick 3–5 topics the brand owns (from § 2). Every post maps to one.
- **Repurpose.** One deep piece (talk, long video, article) becomes many short ones
  (clips, carousels, quotes, threads).
- **Consistency over intensity.** A steady cadence the team can keep beats bursts.
- **Measure what matters.** Saves and shares signal real usefulness; likes are noise.

## 5. Carousels (Instagram / LinkedIn)

Chris Do's carousels are a well-known format: a big, bold hook on slide 1, one idea per
slide in clean, highly readable type, a payoff, and a single CTA.

Template (7–10 slides):
1. **Hook** — a tension, a contrarian claim, a number ("Most X fail at Y. Here's why.").
2. **Stakes** — why the reader should care right now.
3–8. **Value** — one point per slide, short sentence + supporting line. Each slide makes
   the reader want the next one.
9. **Payoff / summary** — the takeaway in one line, worth saving.
10. **CTA** — one action (save, share, follow, comment a word, visit link).

Rules: no clickbait the content can't pay off (it burns trust), lots of white space,
consistent visual system across slides.

## 6. Copy (landing pages, ads, email)

- Headline = the outcome for the specific reader. Sub-head = how / for whom.
- Problem → why it hurts → the different way → proof → one CTA.
- Sell the hole, not the drill: outcomes and transformation, not features or hours.
- Write like you talk to one person across a table.

## 7. Pricing and sales

- **Value-based, not hourly.** Clients pay for outcomes, not time. Price against the
  value of the result to the client.
- **Ask, don't pitch.** In a sales conversation, ask questions until you understand the
  real problem and what solving it is worth; then propose.
- **Price bracketing / anchoring.** Give a range or options, higher first, so the talk
  becomes about value instead of a haggle over one number. Three options (good / better /
  best) let the client choose how much value they want.
- **No neediness.** Work from a "with or without you" confidence built on real skill;
  needy sellers discount and attract bad clients.
